A performance engineer's site should be able to show its own numbers. This page shows them from three vantage points, following a request outwards: the lab, where every deploy is measured before it goes live; the CDN, which times every page it serves; and readers' browsers, which report what they actually got. Figures are published as measured, and refreshed every day.

## Real readers

Each page measures its own Core Web Vitals in the reader's browser, with Google's [web-vitals](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/web-vitals) library, and sends them when the reader leaves. The figures below cover the last 28 days, like Google's own field data.

{{< field_report part="field" />}}

The measurements travel as the address of a request to this site, which CloudFront answers with an empty response and records in the same access logs as everything else. No cookie, no identifier, nothing stored in the browser. The logs keep neither IP addresses nor browser details, and are deleted after 35 days.

## What keeps it light

- **No client-side framework.** Pages are static HTML generated by [Zola](https://www.getzola.org). The only JavaScript is a few small scripts: search, theme switcher, copy buttons, and the measurement above.
- **Fonts cut down to what the site uses.** Inter and Source Serif are subset to Latin: 198 kB instead of 411 kB, and only articles load the serif. Both are preloaded, so text is painted in its final font instead of shifting when it arrives.
- **HTML served from the edge.** CloudFront keeps every page at the point of presence nearest to the reader and serves it compressed (Brotli or gzip), over HTTP/2 or HTTP/3. Each deploy purges that cache.
- **The next page is fetched before the click.** When a link is hovered, the browser prefetches its page ([Speculation Rules](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Speculation_Rules_API)).

## What these numbers don't say

The lab figures come from an emulated mid-range phone on a slow 4G connection, on a GitHub Actions runner, against a local copy of the site: they describe how the pages are built. The edge figures stop at CloudFront, and the trip between the reader and the edge is not in them; they also count bots and crawlers. The readers' figures are the real experience, but only from browsers that run JavaScript and report the metrics: Chromium reports all five, Safari and Firefox fewer. Analytics (Umami) and comments (giscus) are left out of the lab runs.
